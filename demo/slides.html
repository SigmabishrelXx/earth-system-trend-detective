The case

Is Mongolia's climate really changing?
Finding out is harder than it sounds.

Why it's hard

  • Hundreds of Earth variables. Which ones are changing?
  • One process can push a variable up in one region and down in another.
  • A few warm years can look like a trend. Is it statistically significant, or chance?

Why it matters in Mongolia

  • Dzud: harsh winters after dry summers can kill millions of livestock.
  • Desertification and dust storms in the Gobi.
  • Permafrost thaw in the north.

Herders, students and cities need evidence, not guesses.

Built for: high-school and university students, and curious citizens. No coding needed.

How it works

Pick a variable. Pick a place. Get a verdict.

The real app: demo mission 1, "Is Mongolia warming?". Students guess first, then see the evidence.
1
What?14 NASA variables, from temperature and frost days to soil moisture, snow, wind and sunlight. Each with a simple definition.
2
Where?Any point on Earth, 5 Mongolian regions, or all of Mongolia (587 grid cells).
3
When?Whole year or one season, any years from 1981 to 2025.
4
Is it real?t-test with 95% confidence interval, Mann-Kendall test and Sen's slope. "Significant" only when both tests agree.

What we found · NASA POWER, 1981-2025

Mongolia is warming, but not evenly

+0.18 °C
per decade, Mongolia average.
Significant (Mann-Kendall p = 0.005).
62%
of Mongolia's area is warming significantly
(55% after correcting for 587 tests).
-0.04 m/s
per decade: wind is slowing on average across
Mongolia (significant), but not everywhere.
Seasons tell different stories: summers warm significantly at 3 of 5 sites; winters lean colder everywhere, but not significantly.

Opposite trends: Svalbard winters (one Arctic grid point) warmed +1.39 °C per decade (significant) while Ulaanbaatar winters lean -0.24 °C (not significant).
